Broadcom (AVGO) — balance sheet, M&A capacity, AI financing & the multiple
Broadcom went from "de-lever after VMware to fund the next big deal" to "lend its A-rated balance sheet so its two largest 2027–28 customers can buy its chips." Corporate leverage is the lowest in twelve years; the risk moved off balance sheet. Meanwhile the multiple stopped tracking growth in 2026 for the first time in five years — and the M&A machine that defined the company has been idle since 2023.

What the work has established
  • Broadcom is an estimate story, not a multiple story. Over five years the stock is up 647% and the multiple contributed 25 points of it — 89% of the move is forward EPS. But in 2026 the link snapped: EPS +69% YTD, multiple 42.1x → 20.4x, and the correlation with expected growth flipped from +0.73 to −0.79.
  • The M&A machine has no failures in it. Seven deals, twenty years, no impairment. Every deal collapses ROIC to 6–9% in the close year and earns it back past the prior peak within two to four years. The multiple paid is never the multiple underwritten — 13x–42x headline against 8–10x post-synergy.
  • Leverage is not the constraint and has not been since FY24. In the harshest reading of the rating math the balance sheet still funds an $82B all-cash deal; with stock in the mix it stops binding entirely. What has run out is not the capacity but the shopping list.

The one thing S&P said about the backstop

On Jun 11 2026 S&P called the first XPV tranche "credit negative" — with no rating action. Per BofA, S&P leans to treat the guarantee as debt. That characterisation is second-hand and finding the primary text is still open.

Broadcom's own 10-Q added a new risk factor (p.51): downgrade risk "including due to backstops or guarantees and other financing arrangements."

Pending M&A10-Q liquidity section

None disclosed. The liquidity section keeps generic "potential acquisitions" language. The last moves in either direction were the VMware EUC sale ($3.5B, 2024) and the Seagate SoC purchase ($600M, 2024).

Goodwill has been flat at $97.8B since Q1 FY24 — the longest pause in the company's history as a serial acquirer. Against that, Q3 FY26 cash is $24B and net leverage 0.72x. The capacity has never been larger and the pipeline has never been emptier — which is what the M&A capacity section exists to price.

What management says vs what worries
Management P

CFO: partners "independently underwrite and capitalize the assets… we may provide modest residual value guarantees… low risk." Future tranches are "deal-by-deal", "nothing to announce."

Hock Tan (GS): "This is not circular financing." The economics: 1 GW → ~$30B of ARR for the lab against ~$10B/yr to run it.

Roadmap: Anthropic 1 GW 2026 → +5 GW 2027 → +10 GW 2028, the largest XPU customer from 2027. OpenAI 1.3 GW 2027 → >5 GW 2028. Meta 3 GW through 2028.

AI revenue guide: FY26 $58B · FY27 ~$115B · FY28 ~$230B; "on target to exceed $30 EPS in FY28."

Concerns C

1. The two customers that needed financing are the two largest of 2027–28. Much of the FY27/FY28 guide rests on weak-balance-sheet counterparties.

2. The label undersells it. It is a guarantee of 85% of lease payments collateralised by racks — with wrong-way risk: if Anthropic defaults, AI compute is likely in glut and rack values fall.

3. On the Q2 call (Jun 3) Tan said of a backstop "it wasn't backstop in that sense", and on racks "No rack. It's all chips." Five days later they signed a backstop on AI racks.

4. Broadcom books revenue on chips funded by a third party under Broadcom's guarantee — an accounting sale, economically close to vendor financing.

The counter-intuitive result

VMware, the largest deal in software history, was the second-easiest to digest. It peaked at 2.79x — below LSI (4.55x, on a $6.6B deal), below Broadcom Corp (4.25x) and well below Symantec (4.18x). Two reasons, both in the tables above: 62% of the consideration was stock, and the EBITDA base absorbing the debt was already $20B. The company that bought VMware was not the company that bought CA.

CA's count is contaminated: Symantec closed twelve months later, before CA had de-levered. Read them as a single $29.6B software campaign that took 11 quarters — by far the slowest stretch, and the only time Tan went to the well twice before finishing.

The one explicit threshold on the record

In its September 17 2025 upgrade to A−, S&P said it would consider a further upgrade if Broadcom "consistently outgrows the overall IT industry and maintains net leverage below 2.5x through acquisitions and shareholder returns."

Note what that is not: it is not a downgrade trigger. It is the level at which S&P would still go up. The downgrade tolerance sits above it, and the record says where — Broadcom carried 3.7x at BBB−/Baa3, the bottom rung of investment grade.

Net leverageWhat it implies
≤ 2.5xcompatible with an upgrade to A — S&P's own words
~3.0xthe A−/A3 rung holds; the upgrade path is parked
~3.7xBBB−/Baa3 — three to four notches down, the 2018–2020 experience

A deal that takes leverage to 3.7x is not "still investment grade". It is handing back five years of ratings work — which, with a $29B guarantee outstanding and a new risk factor about downgrades "including due to backstops or guarantees", is a different decision than it was in 2018.

FY27 is the fork

Cross-check: the BBG margin ladder puts EBITDA margin at ~67%, and 67% × $105.9B = $70.9B. Two routes agree, so ~$70B is solid for FY26.

But $116B of FY27 EBITDA is consensus — and the multiple section's finding is that the market is currently declining to pay for exactly that number. Using it to size debt capacity would assume away the live debate. Everything below is run on both bases; FY26 is the one to believe.

Read the most punitive cell first
  • FY26 EBITDA only, the backstop and a follow-on guarantee both counted as debt, held to the leverage S&P says is compatible with an upgrade: Broadcom can still write an $82B all-cash cheque. A second VMware, in cash, in the worst corner of the grid.
  • And that is before stock. VMware was 62% stock. Re-run the 2.5x constraint with that mix and leverage stops binding at any deal size that exists — the cash portion grows more slowly than the acquired EBITDA.
  • Interest coverage doesn't bind either. $100B of new debt at ~5.5% is $5.5B against ~$70B of EBITDA; coverage stays above 10x.
  • And it doesn't need to borrow to accumulate. At Q3's 46% FCF margin, FY26 free cash flow is ~$47B against a $12.7B dividend — ~$34B of surplus a year. Three years of retained FCF alone funds a $100B deal with no new debt.
The binding constraint is the target list

At the 9x underwritten multiple, a $150B deal needs a target capable of ~$17B of post-synergy EBITDA. At the ~77% segment operating margin Broadcom has achieved in software, that is a target with roughly $20–25B of revenue.

The problem is not the cheque. It is what Tan pays on revenue — and he has never once bought a high-multiple growth asset.

The list of enterprise-software franchises with $20B+ of revenue, genuine switching costs, an under-priced renewal base and a 3–5x revenue multiple is close to empty in 2026. The same AI re-rating that took Broadcom from 16x to 42x and back also took every plausible target out of Tan's historical range.

Where the backstop actually bites

The $29B backstop is close to irrelevant on its own: it moves net debt from $37.1B to $66.1B, which on FY26E EBITDA is 0.94x instead of 0.72x. EBITDA would have to fall to $26.4B for net debt plus the backstop to reach 2.5x — it was $34.1B in FY25 and is tracking ~$62.5B in FY26. The backstop alone cannot threaten the rating.

The full platform is a different question. Taking BofA's $370B peak-exposure estimate for all 20 GW by mid-2029:

To stay under 2.5x requiresvs consensus
$162.8B of EBITDAFY28E ~$152B → 2.68x, above the line
FY29E ~$210B → 1.94x, back under
Which is the same uncertainty, twice

If the entire 20 GW platform is built and the agencies treat all of it as debt, Broadcom crosses 2.5x on FY28 numbers and comes back under on FY29. Both halves of that sentence are load-bearing — neither the full platform nor the full consensus is committed.

And notice what it means: the backstop is only a rating problem if the FY27–28 AI revenue doesn't arrive. If it arrives, the guarantee is comfortably carried. If it doesn't, it is being called at the same moment the EBITDA is missing. The backstop and the multiple are pricing the same uncertainty.

The conclusion
  • Leverage is not the constraint and has not been since FY24. In the harshest corner of the grid Broadcom can still buy an $82B target for cash; with stock the constraint disappears.
  • Retained cash flow alone is a ~$100B deal in three years, with zero incremental debt.
  • The $29B backstop costs ~0.2x of leverage. A rounding error against the capacity. The follow-on vehicle is what matters, and only at full 20 GW scale, and only if the EBITDA misses.
  • The binding constraint is the target list, not the balance sheet. Never more than 5.4x revenue, never a growth asset. Very little at $20B+ of revenue is available in that range in 2026.
  • Which reframes the multiple question. If "serial acquirer" optionality was part of what the market paid for, the balance sheet is not what took it away — it has never been more capable. Price did. That is a different, and more durable, reason for a lower multiple than a financing scare.

Known gaps in what is already built

CA's underwritten multiple — Broadcom never disclosed a standalone CA EBITDA or synergy target, only the ~$11.6B combined LTM adjusted EBITDA. Backing out Broadcom's own standalone figure from the Q3 FY18 non-GAAP reconciliation would close it.
