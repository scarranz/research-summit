## The workbook
`SPX BBG`: one row per security × year-end snapshot, 11 snapshots (30-Dec-1994 … 31-Dec-2004) × 500 names. Each
window is the calendar year after its snapshot (1995 … 2005). Market cap at the snapshot → opening weight; window
prices and return (**price return**, splits adjusted); BICS sector / industry group / industry. `MEMBERSHIP`: per FIGI
the delisting type (`ACTV`, `ACQU`, `DLST`, `LIQU`, `UNLS`, `PRIV`) and date, plus today's S&P 500 members. **The key is
the FIGI, never the name or ticker**; dead names carry dummy tickers (`0111145D`) that the page never shows.

## What the page computes (everything in the text is computed from the data at load time)
- **Breadth per window**: cap-weighted (fixed-basket) return, equal-weighted, median stock, % of names up, % beating the index, dispersion (σ), best / worst tenth, top-10 weight. The same function is applied to today's SPY year-end holdings (`js/spx-holdings-data.js`, 2020 – 2026 YTD) so both eras use identical measures. Caveat: SPY weights are float-adjusted, the 1990s ones full market cap.
- **Terminal state** per FIGI: `SPX` (in today's S&P 500) · `LIVE` (ACTV, not in the S&P 500) · `ACQ` (ACQU) · `BK` (delisted with a letters + `Q` ticker, `LIQU`, or five hand-listed: Laidlaw, Silicon Graphics, Harnischfeger, Handleman, Armstrong) · `OTH` (other delistings: mergers into a new entity, tracking stocks, privatisation). "Acquired" includes mergers of equals: the business lives on in the buyer; it counts as the name no longer existing, not as a failure. BK counts the **old equity** (Kodak, Delta, American Airlines emerged; their old shares did not).
- **Four stages** (cards) + **every year** (table: S&P 500 published, average and median stock, % of names up, % beating the index, top-10 weight, names that make up half the index, Fed funds / 10-year / 10y-2y curve in December) + three charts (S&P 500 monthly, rates, curve) with a reading box. Stages are only a grouping of the yearly rows. Bands: red = S&P 500 peak to low, grey = NBER recession months (FRED `USREC`). The S&P line is Shiller's monthly series = the monthly average of daily closes; it agrees with the daily Yahoo closes within 0.22% in all 144 months (checked Oct 19 2026). Rates and curve: FRED `FEDFUNDS`, `DGS10`, `T10Y2Y` (monthly average).
- **What happened when** (`EVT`, `READ` in `js/market-history.js`): one line per event, open for the description and a "what to take from it" note (interpretation, labelled as such; numbers inside it are computed from the data). **No S&P 500 reaction is shown**: it implied causality (decided with the analyst, Oct 19 2026). `js/market-history-sp.js` (daily closes, `scripts/market-history/fetch_sp_daily.py`) is still used for the peak / low facts.
- **Index Analysis sections for 1995-2005** (`js/mh-ia.js`): Sector Return / Contribution, Attribution Detail & Simulation, Sector Summary - Market Beaters, Top Contributors and Winners vs Losers, **in the same format as Market Analysis > Index Analysis** (same markup and CSS classes as `spx-sector-alpha.js`, `ma-detail.js`, `market-analysis.js`, `spx-securities.js`; Winners vs Losers calls `distribution()` in `ma-charts.js`, which now takes optional wider bins). They are copies fed with this study's data, not shared code: **if Index Analysis changes its format, change `mh-ia.js` too**. Data: the members at the first trading day of each year, their weight and price return (a name that stopped trading is carried at its last close); contribution = weight x return; sectors and groups = Bloomberg's today applied to every year; "Not explained" = published index minus the members. Each section has its own year picker. The sector section opens on 1998-2002 ("All years" is one button). In Beaters each year is a row of four fixed buttons, Positive / Negative (price return above / below 0) and Beat / Lag (above / below the published S&P 500), one of each pair per year, years combined; a year counts only for a name that was a member the whole year (did not stop trading in it); with years filtered, two extra columns follow the matching names after the last filtered year: median price return in the next 3 years (to Dec 2005) and the share still in the S&P 500 today.
- **Index Composition**: the dumbbell (`dumbbell()` in `js/ma-charts.js`) with Index Analysis year buttons (open to close of a year) OR, with the **Customize** button on, any span from / to (Customize on disables the year buttons, off disables the selects); sector order fixed (by the latest weights).
- **Follow the names** (`js/mh-attrib.js`, under Top Contributors): the best or worst 5 / 10 / 20 contributors of a year picked (own year buttons, default 1999), their price return in EVERY year 1995-2005 (italic = outside the index, Bloomberg price), running share of the index return, today's fate with the year it left, and the median of the group / median stock / published S&P 500 as reference rows. A toggle shows each year as the price return or as the contribution to the index return in pp (weight at the start of the year x return; the reference rows then become the sum of the group and the published return). The scatter notes how it differs from this view.
- **Prices outside the index (`pe`)**: `outside snapshots dot com analysis/bloomberg_prices_needed_by_date.xlsx` (one sheet, one block per year-end Dec 1994 to Dec 2005: BBG ticker, name, why, close, FIGI). Pulled Oct 16 2026 for 386 names: the year-ends BEFORE a name joined the S&P 500, AFTER it left while still trading (delisting date later than the exit), and the gap of US Bancorp; 1,999 cells, 1,602 prices, 397 `#N/A N/A` (not listed yet / already delisted; 44 names have no price at any requested date). Read by `read_outside()` in the extractor into `pe` (12 entries, same index as `p`). Checks: NA pattern clean (never an NA after a price while listed); price jumps at joining plausible (e.g. QUALCOMM x27 in 1999); 11 of 20 names compared with Yahoo Finance close equal to the cent, the other 9 differ by Bloomberg's spin-off / special-cash adjustment, as the existing workbook prices do (e.g. AIG). Not an overlap test: no date was requested twice. `px()` in the page uses `p`, else `pe`.
- **Did yesterday's winners stay winners?** (scatter): every name with a price at the start date and at the split date (in the S&P 500 or not); x = price change in the N years BEFORE the split date, y = the M years AFTER (selects: split date, years before, years after; default Dec 1999, 5 and 3). **The axes never change**: both run -95% to +20,000% on a ratio scale, a name beyond a limit sits on the edge. The N best / worst by x (10 / 20 / 50) are strong green / red, everyone else near-transparent grey; a name that stops trading stays at its last close.
- **The euphoria's winners and laggards: what happened next**: the top and bottom fifth of the members at Dec 1999 by 1995-99 price return (`q` in the data, 91 + 92 names), each split by what they did in 2000-05 against the S&P 500 (beat it / fell behind it / lost more than 75%), with the count, median return, share still in the S&P 500 today, names gone by 2005, main sectors and largest names. **An outcome and sector split only**: profit and revenue of each name at the time are not in the data. To test "real earnings vs smoke" the next step is `fundamentals_needed_dec1999.xlsx` (net income and revenue, FY0 and FY-1, for those 183 names; one table, a few hundred cells).
- **Names added to the index**: for each year-end, the names that appear for the first time: median return in the year BEFORE joining (Bloomberg price a year earlier, `pe`), in the year after, and the names already in the index that year. Pooled medians in the reading line. No SPAC-type vehicles were looked for beyond a name scan; the universe is S&P 500 members only.
- **Where today differs** (`D.now`, built by `read_now()` in the extractor from `macro/now/`): Dec 1999 | Mar 2000 (S&P peak) | latest, for the Fed funds rate, 10-year yield, 10y-2y curve, monetary base / GDP (FRED `BOGMBASE`), non-financial company debt / GDP (`BCNSDODNS`), federal debt / GDP (`GFDEGDQ188S`), margin debt / GDP (FINRA), Shiller CAPE and trailing earnings yield, inflation (`CPIAUCSL`, 12 months), corporate profits after tax / GDP (`CP`), unemployment (`UNRATE`), Baa-10y spread (`BAA10Y`), plus top-10 weight and names for half the index (study data vs the SPY holdings in the portal, float-adjusted). "Different" = moved by at least a fixed margin per measure (`TOL`, printed under the table); it is a rule, not a view on risk. Latest = last complete month / latest published quarter. **No analyst view or opinion text** (removed Oct 19 2026: it was not tested by any data).
- **Take away**: one computed paragraph, what the data shows (six points with their numbers) and what it does not show (the differences and similarities with today are taken from the table above).

## Caveats the page states
1. S&P 500 members only. A name that leaves the index is carried to its **last close** (that year's return includes the exit) and nothing replaces it; the data does not follow it after that. Year-end snapshots (last trading day) are the open and close of each window, so there is no intra-year data.
2. Fixed basket per year: intra-year entries / exits and weight drift are not modelled (see the 2000 gap).
3. Peak = highest **year-end** price observed, not the March 2000 high.
4. BICS ≠ GICS (today's side of the concentration comparison uses GICS Information Technology + Communication Services, comparable not identical).
5. Market-cap weights until the 2005 float adjustment (S&P announced it in 2005; completed Sep 2005).
6. Event dates on the macro charts were checked on Oct 16 2026 against: Federal Reserve press releases / FOMC minutes (Sep 29, Oct 15, Nov 17 1998; May 16 2000; 1.00% by Jun 25 2003, CRS), S&P DJI and press reports (S&P 500 close 1,527.46 on Mar 24 2000 and 776.76 on Oct 9 2002; Nasdaq Composite 5,048.62 on Mar 10 2000), public laws (Telecom Act Feb 8 1996; Taxpayer Relief Act Aug 5 1997, top long-term rate 28% → 20% for assets held over 18 months; Gramm-Leach-Bliley Nov 12 1999, P.L. 106-102; Sarbanes-Oxley Jul 30 2002, P.L. 107-204) and the bankruptcy filings (Enron Dec 2 2001, WorldCom Jul 21 2002). Rule: no figure goes on the page unless it comes from the data or a checked source.
   Added Oct 16 2026: NATO air campaign in Kosovo 24 Mar - 10 Jun 1999 (nato.int), US operations in Afghanistan from 7 Oct 2001 and the Iraq invasion from 20 Mar 2003 (US DoD casualty site; some sources date Iraq 19 Mar by time zone). The Gulf War (1991) is outside the window; no US war with Iran or Pakistan in it.
7. The file ends in 2005: nothing is said about what the rotation of 2000-05 led to.
