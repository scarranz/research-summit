Two companies own the toll booth. Buying them is not the same trade twice.
The technology overview ended at a single conclusion: no robot OEM makes its own reducer, and the two mechanisms that matter are owned by two Japanese firms that split the robot's body rather than compete for it. This is the high-level read on both — what each company actually is underneath the component, where the earnings really come from, and what you are exposed to when you own it. The duopoly, in one screen
complementary, not rivals
They mostly do not bid for the same slot: Nabtesco owns the heavy proximal joints (base, shoulder, elbow) with cycloidal RV; Harmonic owns the fine distal joints (wrists, fingers, hands) with strain-wave. A robot arm contains both. But as securities they are opposites — Nabtesco is a diversified industrial that happens to own the toll booth; Harmonic is the toll booth with nothing else attached.

The one-line difference

Nabtesco gives you the widest, most designed-in tollbooth in the industry — wrapped in trains and doors that dilute it. Harmonic gives you undiluted exposure to the fine joint — and an income statement that went to essentially zero operating profit one year ago. The ballast you want in a drawdown is the same ballast that caps the upside you're buying the theme for.

How to read these two together

They are not substitutes in a portfolio. Nabtesco is the quality-and-breadth expression of the reducer thesis (own the toll, accept dilution); Harmonic is the high-beta expression (own the theme purely, accept a ~100% earnings swing and a single-country listing). The technology says both are necessary; the financials say they are two different decisions.

The moat — what protects it, where it ends
stated honestly in both directions
✓ What protects it

~60% share + the Teijin Seiki cycloidal heritage + 7M+ units of field history · designed-in with all four Big-Four OEMs (a quasi-tollbooth on industrial-robot growth) · anchor legs (rail / aero / doors) with certification barriers and recurring service · scale, qualification and integrated bearing design.

✕ Where it ends

The robotics segment is cyclical, with soft Chinese demand · RV may not travel outside industrial robots — it is heavy for humanoids, and Optimus uses roller screws for heavy leg joints · Chinese makers press on price and are expanding into cycloidal · the aero leg is exposed to Boeing 737 MAX production rates.

Where the inflection actually shows

Q4 FY3/26 ordinary profit ¥1.28B (+5.6× YoY), operating margin 2.2% → 7.9%. On the balance sheet, receivables +21.2% while liabilities fell 10.6% — volume returning before the P&L reflects it. But note the market's read: on the FY3/26 print the stock fell −7.75% the next day before rebounding, and the payout ratio is 117.7% — it is paying out more than it earns.

The product ladder — the strategic answer to China
the higher the rung, the harder to price-compare
Climbing the ladder is the whole China defence: a bare component can be compared datasheet against datasheet; a calibrated joint cannot.
Why rung 3 is the strategic core

Switching supplier on a rotary actuator means re-engineering the motor-gear coupling, thermal behaviour, control tuning and mounting. This is where humanoid orders concentrate — because the developers are software companies, not precision-mechanics companies, and they want a finished joint.

Why rung 2 matters technically

Strain-wave units are assembly-sensitive: a misalignment degrades exactly the backlash you paid for. Selling a pre-mounted gear unit lets Harmonic guarantee the spec in the field, not in the lab.

The two concentration problems
customer, and owner
🔴 Nissan — an asymmetric dependency

10.30% of Harmonic's revenue comes from Nissan; Harmonic is 0.05% of Nissan's COGS. If Nissan cuts capex or reprices, Harmonic absorbs it with no leverage to resist — and it is automotive revenue sitting inside a robotics narrative. Only 12 customers and 10 suppliers are disclosed at all, and two counterparties (Nachi-Fujikoshi, Nanyo) are both supplier and customer — so "10 / 12" overstates the real breadth. Nachi-Fujikoshi also supplies Nabtesco: a shared node of the Japanese complex, where one disruption touches both incumbents.

⚠️ Koden Holdings is the PARENT — not a supplier

SPLC lists Koden as a supplier (Commercial Support Services). It is in fact the parent, holding 34.77%. Free float is 57.68% — reasonable, not a suffocating cross-shareholding — but Koden has effective control, and Koden + HDS acted jointly in the Hataken acquisition (Oct-2024), another name that appears as a "supplier". Lesson: SPLC classifies by commercial flow, not by control — always verify ownership separately. T. Rowe (4.3%), Capital Research (3.2%) and FIL are already holders, so there is precedent for building size.

The correct framing

✕ "Harmonic wins if humanoids scale."
✓ "Harmonic wins if humanoids scale AND Western platforms choose qualified Japanese supply over Chinese cost." Today Tesla's Optimus uses Green Harmonic (China), and Leaderdrive is in at Tesla, UBTECH and Figure AI — so the second condition is currently failing, not passing.

The barbell to remember

One end is protected but slow — semiconductors, aerospace/defence (Moog, BAE), medical, where field history decides and failure is catastrophic. The other end is fast but under siege — industrial robots and humanoids, exactly where China attacks hardest and where no incumbency protects. The question is whether the growth arrives in the tier Harmonic still dominates. The screen is unsentimental about it: over the last 3M, Estun +57.8% outran Harmonic +49.8% — the market is not pricing the Chinese as losers.

Accounting note to carry into any comparison

Both report under J-GAAP. Japanese operating profit is systematically understated versus IFRS, and net income is inflated in extraordinary years — Harmonic's FY3/25 is the direct trap (a ¥5.78B gain from selling its Nabtesco stake sits below the operating line). Use operating profit, and adjust before any cross-border multiple. Also note "ordinary profit" (経常利益) has no equivalent outside Japan.

Facts that are commonly stated wrong — do not repeat them

✕ "Nabtesco bought Harmonic in 2020" — false. The opposite happened: Harmonic sold its Nabtesco stake (¥5.78B gain, FY3/25). · ✕ "Koden is a supplier" — it is the parent, 34.77%. · ✕ "HSYDF is the ADR" — there is no ADR; it is an unsponsored foreign ordinary.

The distilled question
what the detailed deep-dives have to answer
Not "do you believe in humanoids"

It is: who captures the value — the Japanese moat owner or the Chinese low-cost challenger — and how much of that is already priced. The tension is uncomfortable and should stay on the page: the moat is real, but already penetrated in mid-tier; growth is in mid-tier; premium grows slowly; humanoids carry no incumbency to protect; and Tesla, UBTECH and Figure have already chosen Chinese supply. The moat is strongest where field history matters (slow markets) and weakest where the product is new (the market that supposedly explodes).
