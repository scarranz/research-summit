Oscar Health, Inc. NYSE: OSCR

Built only from Oscar’s own documents: SEC filings (FY2025 10-K, 3Q26 10-Q, the Nov 5, 2026 earnings release) and the Sep 16, 2026 Investor Day materials. No market data, no model, no consensus.

Oscar Health is a US health insurer that sells individual health plans, mostly through the Affordable Care Act (ACA) marketplaces, to about 2.7 million members in 20 states. It runs its insurance business on a technology platform it built itself, covering claims, member service and care navigation, and since 2025 it also owns enrollment and brokerage businesses aimed at employer-funded individual coverage.

What it sells
Individual ACA health plans (Catastrophic to Platinum), mostly exclusive-provider networks, plus an enrollment platform and an insurance brokerage.
Who buys it
Individuals and families on the ACA marketplaces, mostly through brokers, and employees using an employer ICHRA allowance. CMS paid 93% of 2025 premiums as subsidies.
How it earns
FY2025: ~98% premiums · ~1.7% investment income · ~0.2% other. Medical costs took 87.4% of premiums in FY2025 (a loss) and 78.1% in 9M26 (a profit).
The edge our read · to confirm
A pure individual-market insurer on its own technology stack. SG&A fell from 19.1% of revenue (FY2024) to 14.4% (9M26) while membership grew.
How Oscar makes money

FY2025 total revenue $11.7B (+27.5%). Oscar reports a single reportable segment, so there is no segment split; below, the same total by revenue line, where each revenue dollar goes, and where the members are.

Premium (almost all of revenue)

A fixed monthly payment per member for the plan the member chose. Most of it is paid by the federal government (CMS) as a tax-credit subsidy; the member pays the rest. Premium is booked net of risk adjustment: the ACA program moves money from plans with healthier members to plans with sicker ones, and Oscar has been a net payer.

The numbers
Investment income (the float)

Interest and gains on the cash and bonds Oscar holds between collecting premiums and paying claims, mostly inside its regulated insurance subsidiaries, which must keep minimum capital.

The numbers
Other revenues (the marketplace businesses)

Fees from the brokerage, enrollment platform and consumer-education businesses bought in 2025, revenue sharing on virtual credit card rebates, and sublease income. Fees from the +Oscar technology platform were included in FY2025.

The cost lines (what the filing breaks out)

The segment note splits operating cost into medical expenses and three SG&A lines: member acquisition and servicing (broker commissions and member service), premium taxes and exchange fees, and all other SG&A (employees and administration). The two ratios management tracks are the MLR (medical cost ÷ premiums) and the SG&A ratio (SG&A ÷ revenue).

The numbers

FY2025 loss from operations $(396)M and net loss $(443)M, after higher market-wide morbidity raised the risk-adjustment payable. 9M26 earnings from operations $1,271M; on Nov 5, 2026 the company guided FY2026 earnings from operations to $850–950M on revenue of $19.0–19.3B. Oscar collects a monthly premium for each member, pays its members’ medical bills, and keeps what is left after paying brokers, taxes and its own staff.

The business in four steps
1Sign up a member

A person or family picks an Oscar plan on the ACA marketplace, almost always through a broker.

2.69M members · Sep 2026

3Pay for the care

When members see doctors, go to hospital or fill prescriptions, Oscar pays the bill to its network.

Why the profit swings so much
≈ $190M

What one point of medical cost ratio (MLR) is worth in 2026. With FY2026 revenue guided at $19.0–19.3B, almost all premium, each point of premium spent on care moves operating profit by roughly $190M, a fifth of the $850–950M guided. That is why the MLR going from 87.4% to 78.1% turned a loss into a $1.27B nine-month profit. (Our arithmetic on company guidance.)

Prices are set before the year

Premium rates must be approved by state and federal regulators, and Oscar decides each year which markets to enter. If care costs more than priced, Oscar absorbs it until the next year’s rates.

The bill depends on everyone else

Risk adjustment compares Oscar’s members with the whole market in each state. In 2025 the market got sicker than expected, Oscar owed more into the pool, and the year ended in a loss.

Who enrolls changes the math

Subsidy rules decide who buys. Enhanced subsidies expired at the end of 2025 and new eligibility checks apply; a smaller market can be a sicker one.

Oscar wants to go from “the ACA insurer” to the company that leads the individual health market: people, not employers, choosing and buying their own coverage and health products.

Engine 3 · enablerTechnology & AI

Oscar’s own full-stack platform: claims, network, billing, clinical care, member service.

  • Not sold separately

  • 60% of new production code AI-assisted (Aug 2026, from 16% in Jan)
2029 goal

~100% of office & lab visits paid in real time · 90%+ member issues solved first pass

CHOICE is Oscar’s new name for ICHRA: instead of a group plan, the employer gives each worker a fixed amount to buy their own ACA plan. Company example: the employer saves ~15% on contributions, the worker buys a ~$570/month plan and has ~$100/month left for out-of-pocket costs.

What we would watch our read · not company content
  • $4 of EPS needs the upper half of the margin range. On the company’s own assumptions (~$20M interest and other, mid-20s tax rate, 331M diluted shares growing 2–3% a year) and 20% growth from $18.85B (≈$32.6B in 2029), $4.00 needs about a 5.8–5.9% operating margin. At 5% EPS is ≈$3.40; at 7% ≈$4.75.
  • Policy sets the size of the market. The plan already absorbs −2M lives from program integrity, and the CHOICE leg depends on new rules being written.
  • Lucie is still a promise. The value pools are illustrative and there is no revenue target; in the revenue bridge, new products and services are worth 2–4 points of growth.
  • Pricing risk does not go away with scale. Each MLR point is ≈$190M of 2026 profit (see How Oscar works). The 2025 loss came from that single line.
