# Sprouts Farmers Market (NASDAQ: SFM): research notes

As of 2026-11-18. Sources: SEC filings only (10-K, 10-Q, 8-K earnings releases Ex. 99.1, 8-K Item 5.02). Numbers are in `data.json`. Short forms used below: "10-K FY25" means the 10-K for fiscal 2025 (acc. 0001575515-26-000008), "Q3'26 8-K" means the Oct 28, 2026 earnings release (acc. 0001575515-26-000059), and so on.

## (a) What Sprouts is and how it makes money

**What it sells.** Sprouts is a specialty grocer selling fresh, natural and organic food. Produce sits in the middle of the store, which is the opposite of a normal supermarket. Produce takes about 20% of selling space, which the company says is far more than most peers. The rest of the store is packaged grocery, meat and seafood, deli, bakery, dairy, bulk bins, frozen, vitamins and supplements, and body care. The range leans toward organic, plant-based and gluten-free products and toward "attribute-driven" items such as keto, paleo and non-GMO (10-K FY25, Item 1).

**Store format.** The open layout with low shelves is meant to feel like a farmers market. The current fleet averages about 28,000 sq ft (27,237 total sq ft per store at FY25 year-end). Since 2020, new stores use a smaller box of 21,000–25,000 sq ft. These are cheaper to build, have less back-room space, and cost less to occupy and run. Each store has 75–100 team members (10-K FY25). The company had 477 stores in 24 states at FY25 year-end and 504 stores in 25 states at 2026-09-27. California is the largest market with 156 stores, a third of the chain (10-K FY25).

**Target customer.** Since the 2020 strategy, the 10-Ks name two target groups. The first is **"health enthusiasts."** The second was called **"experience seekers"** in 10-K FY20 and was renamed **"selective shoppers"** from 10-K FY23 on. 10-K FY25 says it was "formerly referred to as experience seekers." The filings never use the term "innovation seeker." Innovation and "foraging" for new products are how Sprouts targets the second group.

**How it makes money.**
- **Gross margin is 38.8% (FY25), up from about 33.6% in 2016–2019.** On the current accounting basis, cost of sales covers product and supply-chain costs only. Store occupancy and buying costs sit in SG&A. SG&A is about 29% of sales and D&A about 1.7%, which leaves an operating margin of **7.8% in FY25**. That was the best in the 11 years covered here. The low point was 3.9% in FY19. (10-K FY25 MD&A; derived ratios in `data.json`.)
- **Perishables are about 57% of sales.** These are produce, meat, seafood, deli, bakery, floral and dairy (10-K FY25 revenue note). Selling perishables well depends on shrink control, meaning less spoiled or lost product. The FY25 gross-margin gain of 70 bp was attributed to "improved shrink and our investments in inventory management" (10-K FY25). The Q3'26 margin decline of 50 bp was mostly higher shrink (+0.4 pt of sales) as comps softened (10-Q Q3'26).
- **Private label ("Sprouts Brand")** was "just over 25%" of revenue in FY25, up from "over 10%" in FY16 (10-K FY16, 10-K FY25). The filings say private label offers "lower prices while still delivering generally higher margin" than branded goods (10-K FY20).
- **Supply chain.** Sprouts runs its own fresh distribution centers (DCs). There are six: two in CA, plus AZ, TX, CO and FL. In 2025 it began distributing its own meat and seafood through four of these DCs, covering about 70% of stores, and expects about 95% coverage in 2026. Dry grocery and frozen come mainly from **KeHE (52% of total purchases in FY25)**, with UNFI as the second distributor (12% in FY25, up from 3%) (10-K FY25).
- **Capital allocation.** Free cash flow of about $470m in FY25 went largely to buybacks: $472m in FY25. Diluted shares fell from 155.9m (FY15) to 98.7m (FY25), and buybacks continued in 2026. The revolver has been undrawn since mid-2024 (Q2'24 8-K; 10-K FY25).

## (b) Strategy history

**2015–2018: fast growth, falling margins.** Doug Sanders moved from CEO to Executive Chairman in Aug 2015. Amin Maredia became CEO and Jim Nielsen President/COO (8-K 2015-08-10; 8-K 2016-05-25). Sprouts opened 27–36 stores a year and targeted 14% unit growth (10-K FY15). Comps slowed from 5.8% (FY15) to 2.1% (FY18). Operating margin fell from 6.4% to 4.3%. 10-K FY17 cited a "competitive environment in the first half of 2017." In Q4 FY18 the company changed how it classifies expenses (occupancy and buying costs moved to SG&A, and D&A got its own line). This makes gross margin before FY16 incomparable (10-K FY18, Note 3).

**Late 2018–mid 2019: leadership gap.** Maredia resigned effective Dec 30, 2018 (8-K 2018-12-04). Nielsen and CFO Brad Lukow were interim co-CEOs (8-K 2018-12-26). Nielsen then took medical leave (8-K 2019-05-02).

**June 2019: Jack Sinclair becomes CEO.** He came from 99 Cents Only, where he was CEO, and before that ran Walmart US Grocery (8-K 2019-06-26). Denise Paulonis joined as CFO in Feb 2020 (8-K 2020-01-07). The reset began with **promotions**: "promotional activities started in the fourth quarter of 2019" (10-K FY20). The Feb 2020 outlook explicitly left out strategic changes (Q4'19 8-K).

**May 2020: the long-term strategy** (Q1'20 8-K, 2020-05-05; 10-K FY20). It had five pillars:
1. **Win with target customers** (health enthusiasts / experience seekers) and expand ecommerce.
2. **Smaller format and market expansion.** Smaller boxes, placed where target customers live, with "a long runway of at least 10% annual unit growth."
3. **Advantaged fresh supply chain.** Fresh DCs within a **250-mile radius** of stores.
4. **Refine brand and marketing.** Less reliance on broad promotions and more digital and social engagement. Net advertising spend fell from $57.2m (FY19) to $45.9m (FY21) while sales grew (10-K notes).
5. **Financial targets.** New-store build cost about 20% lower, about 40% cash-on-cash returns, 10%+ unit growth, low single-digit comps, stable-to-expanding EBIT margin, and low double-digit EPS growth.

**COVID (FY20, a 53-week year).** COVID added an estimated $146m to Q1'20 sales and 9.6 pts to that quarter's comps (Q1'20 8-K). Ecommerce was 13% of net sales in April 2020 and ecommerce sales rose 340% in 2020 (8-Ks 2020-05-05, 2021-02-25). FY20 gross margin rose 3.2 pts to 36.8%, "due to strategic changes in promotional activities in addition to shrink favorability" plus COVID volume (10-K FY20). Buybacks were paused (zero in FY20). FY21 comps were −6.7% as the company cycled the COVID surge. Openings fell to 12 because of equipment-supply delays, and the 10% unit-growth start moved from 2022 to 2023 (10-K FY21).

**2021–2023: building the network.** CO and FL DCs opened in 2021, bringing more than 85% of stores within 250 miles of a DC (10-K FY21). In 2023 the GA DC closed and the SoCal DC relocated (10-K FY23). Sprouts closed 11 underperforming stores in 2023 after a real-estate review, with $27.8m of impairments (10-K FY23). It also closed 4 stores in 2022. Chip Molloy became CFO in Sep 2021 (8-K 2021-08-31), and Curtis Valentine succeeded him on Jan 1, 2024 (8-K 2023-10-31). Nick Konat has been President/COO since March 2022 (8-K 2026-09-01).

**2024–2025: payoff.** Comps were +7.6% (FY24) and +7.3% (FY25), and openings reached 33 and 37. Quarterly comps peaked at +11.7% in Q1'25. Operating margin rose from 5.1% (FY23) to 7.8% (FY25), and EPS from $2.50 to $5.31. Sprouts Rewards loyalty launched nationwide in 2025 (10-K FY25).

**Leadership transition ahead.** Nick Konat becomes CEO on **Jan 4, 2027**, and Sinclair becomes Executive Chairman (8-K 2026-09-01). Other 2026 changes:
- New Chief Merchandising Officer (Don Clark, ex-Giant Eagle and Whole Foods), replacing the retiring Scott Neal.
- A new Chief Customer Officer role (8-K 2026-02-19).
- The CTO left in Oct 2026 (8-K 2026-10-14).

## (c) Where it stands now

**FY2025 (52 weeks):** sales $8.81bn (+14%), comps +7.3%, 37 openings, gross margin 38.8%, EBIT $686m (7.8%), diluted EPS $5.31. This met or beat the original FY25 guidance of adj. EPS $4.52–4.68 and comps of 4.5–6.5% (Q4'24 8-K; 10-K FY25). Comps slowed through the year: Q3'25 +5.9%, Q4'25 +1.6%.

**FY2026: a reversal.**
- **Original guide (Feb 19, 2026):** comps −1% to +1%, EBIT $675–695m, EPS $5.28–5.44, 40+ stores. The CFO warned of "challenges in 2026, especially in the first half" (Q4'25 8-K).
- **Q1–Q3 2026:** comps −1.7%, −1.0%, −0.9%. Q1 and Q2 EPS came in at the top of their ranges.
- **Q3 2026** (13 weeks to Sep 27): sales $2.30bn (+5%), comps −0.9%. That was below the guided −0.5% to +1.5%, and it included about −0.6 pt from a **cyclospora outbreak** that hit produce (10-Q Q3'26). Gross margin was 38.2% (−50 bp, from shrink and fuel), and EPS $1.21, within the $1.20–1.24 guide. A record 15 stores opened, for 504 stores in total.
- **Guidance cut (Oct 28, 2026).** FY26 (52-week basis) is now about +4.3% sales, about −1.7% comps, about **$605m EBIT** (vs $675–685m in July) and about **$4.80 EPS** (vs $5.32–5.40). Q4 is guided to comps of −2% to −4% and EPS of $0.47–0.51, against $0.92 a year earlier.
- **Why the cut.** Management is "increasing investments to enhance affordability," including "broader and deeper price investments," plus technology and supply chain, "in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 and into fiscal 2027" (Q3'26 8-K; 10-Q Q3'26).
- **Implied Q4.** Q4 EBIT on a 13-week basis works out to about $63m (derived: $605m − $542m 9M), against $123m in Q4'25.
- **53rd week.** FY26 has a 53rd week, now estimated at about $190m of sales, $20m of EBIT and $0.16 of EPS.
- **What did not change:** the unit-growth target (42 net new stores) and capex (about $310m net).

**Balance sheet (2026-09-27):** cash $229m and an undrawn $600m revolver. "Long-term debt and other finance obligations" is $130m, which is finance obligations and finance leases, not bank debt. YTD buybacks were $299m.

## (d) Growth levers management cites (10-K FY25; Q3'26 8-K)

- **Units: about 10% annual growth,** with more than 40 stores in 2026 and more concentrated entry into new markets. Smaller boxes are cheaper to build and earn better returns. Management says recent store vintages perform strongly.
- **Comps from target customers:** innovation and "foraging," Sprouts Brand growth (now above 25% of sales), personalization through the Sprouts Rewards loyalty program, and omnichannel delivery and pickup through Instacart, DoorDash and Uber Eats.
- **Supply chain:** fresh DCs within 250 miles (about 80% of stores today), and meat and seafood self-distribution reaching about 95% of stores in 2026. The goals are fresher product, better fill rates, less shrink and margin.
- **Affordability** (new in Q4'26): price investment to bring traffic back.
- **Technology:** inventory management and data and personalization tools.
- **Capital returns:** a $1bn buyback authorized in Q3 2025.

## (e) Sprouts-specific risks (10-K FY25 Item 1A) and competition

- **Produce dependence and food safety.** Fresh produce is the core of the business. Supply disruptions from weather, disease and grower concentration, product recalls, or illness claims hurt it directly. The 2026 cyclospora outbreak cost about 0.6 pt of Q3'26 comps.
- **Growth depends on new stores.** Sprouts has to find sites and keep landlord contributions, since lower contributions hurt cash-on-cash returns. New stores have lower margins at first and take some sales from older nearby stores.
- **Comps can be volatile.** The 10-K explicitly flags "cycling prior year performance." Comps went from +11.7% in Q1'25 to −1.7% in Q1'26.
- **Shrink** gets its own risk factor. It is the main swing factor in gross margin.
- **Distributor concentration.** KeHE is 52% of purchases under a contract through July 2035. UNFI is 12%, with a contract through July 2026.
- **DC concentration.** Six DCs carry all produce and most meat and seafood. Any interruption cuts supply to many stores.
- **Geographic concentration.** California is 33% of stores, which brings exposure to that state's economy, regulation, weather and wildfires.
- **Labor.** Sprouts has no unions, and the 10-K flags union organizing as a risk. Wage and benefit inflation is another.
- **Competition** (10-K FY25). The industry is fragmented. Named competitors are Whole Foods, Trader Joe's, Kroger, Albertsons/Safeway, H-E-B, Publix, Target, Walmart, warehouse clubs and Amazon. Sprouts says its new stores "capture market share from conventional supermarkets and specialty concepts." It notes that rivals are expanding natural/organic and vitamin ranges, and that rival price cuts in deflationary periods can squeeze margins. The 2026 price investment is the first sign in the filings of Sprouts responding on price.

## (f) Data quirks and comparability warnings

1. **53-week years:** FY2015 (ended 2016-01-03), FY2020 (ended 2021-01-03) and **FY2026** (ends 2027-01-03, extra week in Q4). Annual sales, growth and sales per store are inflated in those years. FY26 guidance is given on a 52-week basis, and Q4'20 comps were reported on a 13-week basis.
2. **2018 change in accounting principle** (10-K FY18, Note 3), applied back to FY2016–17. Occupancy and buying costs moved from cost of sales to SG&A. Pre-opening and direct store expenses were folded into SG&A, and D&A (excluding supply-chain depreciation) became its own line. FY16 gross profit went from $1,182m to $1,363m. **FY2015 exists only on the old basis**, so `gross_profit`/`sga` are null for FY15 and the old-basis lines are in `annual_legacy_basis`. Pre-opening cost is not disclosed after FY2017.
3. **The perishables definition changed in the FY2018 10-K.** Dairy and dairy alternatives (and floral) moved into perishables, so FY16 went from 50.4% to 58.1%. Both series are in `product_mix`.
4. **Square-footage basis changed.** It was "gross" sq ft through FY2020, "selling" sq ft in the FY2022 10-K (FY20–22), and "total sq ft excl. vacant/subleased" from FY2023. FY2020 appears under both bases (10.51m gross vs 10.34m selling).
5. **Private label became "Sprouts Brand"** in the FY2022 10-K, when the share jumped from about 16% to "just over 19%." The definition may have widened, but the filings do not say. All percentages are rounded "approximately / just over / over" figures.
6. **Comp definition:** stores enter the comp base in their 61st week after opening or acquisition and leave on the day they close (10-K FY15, 10-K FY25). It has not changed. The FY2015 10-K labels comps (including FY15's 5.8%) "pro forma," meaning they include stores acquired in the 2011–2012 "Transactions" for all periods. That only matters for years before FY2015.
7. **Capex:** `capex` is gross purchases of property and equipment from the cash-flow statement. The company's releases quote capex "net of landlord reimbursements," which is lower (FY25 $224m net vs $248m gross). FCF here = CFO − gross capex.
8. **Debt:** `total_debt` is revolver borrowings only. Finance leases are shown separately, and operating leases ($1.86bn at FY25) are excluded. FY2025 adds **$69.4m of "finance obligations"** due in 2036. The filings do not explain them; they are probably a failed sale-leaseback or build-to-suit arrangement. FY2019–21 revolver balances come from 10-K text because XBRL `LongTermDebt` is missing for those years.
9. **One store unaccounted for in Q3'26:** 490 stores + 15 openings = 505, but 504 were reported. No closure is disclosed.
10. **FY2023 GAAP EPS of $2.50** includes $30.5m of impairments, mostly for the 11 closures. The company's adjusted EPS was $2.84. "Adjusted" metrics were dropped from guidance in Q1 2025.
11. **Team members** are stated as "approximately" or "more than" round numbers. The `team_members_qualifier` field records which one applies.
12. **Not disclosed anywhere in SEC filings:** sales per store, sales per sq ft, an annual ecommerce % of sales (only April and July 2020 monthly figures exist), and new-store cash-on-cash results versus the 40% target.
