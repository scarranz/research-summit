## Q2 2026 — July 29, 2026

Participants: Mark Zuckerberg (CEO), Susan Li (CFO), Kenneth Dorell (IR). Release 4:05pm ET,
call 5:00pm ET. Same-day Follow-Up Q&A folded in (one call, per §3).

### The print (8-K Ex. 99.1)

- Revenue **$60,801M (+28%)** — top of the $58–61B guide, vs BBG $60.0B / LSEG $60.24B.
- FoA ad revenue $59,363M (+27%; +26% cc); impressions **+14%**, price/ad **+12%**.
- FoA other revenue **$1,007M (+73%)** — first quarter over $1B.
- Operating income **$18,775M (−8%, 31% margin)** — carries **$2.40B legal-proceedings charges +
  $1.18B severance** (May headcount reduction); ex-items ~$22.4B (+9%). Total expenses $42.0B (+55%).

  roughly in line, derived); tax rate 16%.

  Quarterly **FCF $784M**. Cash & mkt securities $90.3B; total debt $83.7B.

- FoA op income $23,394M (carries the legal charges). DAP 3.6B. Headcount ~75,000 (−3% QoQ).
- Guidance: **Q3 2026 revenue $61–64B (~1% FX headwind)** · FY26 expenses **$165–169B** (floor
  raised to absorb the charges) · FY26 capex **narrowed to $130–145B** (floor +$5B) · tax **15–17%**
  for the rest of 2026 · RL losses still framed to peak in 2026.
- After hours: ~−9% (from a $585.61 close). Next-day close to confirm before quoting.

### Q&A — the record

- **Brian Nowak (Morgan Stanley):** which AI revenue line scales first / 2027 capex. Mark: "there
  will continue to be a significantly higher margin on selling intelligence rather than selling
  compute directly"; "meaningful growth in all of these areas… more to share soon." Susan on 2027:
  "infrastructure planning remains highly dynamic… we're still working through what our capacity
  needs are going to be" — **no number, third consecutive ask**; "near-term capacity is more
  valuable than long-term capacity."
- **Eric Sheridan (Goldman):** enterprise muscle / capital sourcing. Mark: extend the ads model to
  business agents ("we will get paid when we deliver results"); "a somewhat different muscle than we
  have historically had… will share more soon." Susan: operating cash flow first, then the debt mix,
  "cost-efficient, long-duration sources of capital."
- **Mark Shmulik (Bernstein):** consumer-agent adoption. Mark: "it's extremely unlikely… five years
  from now that you don't have billions of people with a personal agent"; "it needs to just work";
  "we're great at, once we get something working, scaling it to a large number of people."
- **Doug Anmuth (JPM):** LLM-recsys headroom / compute premium. Susan: "further headroom to continue
  improving recommendations over the rest of the year and into 2027." Mark: "there's just nowhere
  near enough compute for all the demand"; "it would be foolish to basically just sell all of the
  compute and take a short-term profit."
- **Justin Post (BofA):** lab trajectory a year in. Mark: "quite happy with the trajectory"; larger,
  more advanced models "in the process of scaling"; the data flywheel ("a very clear mental model of
  everything going on in the person's life").
- **Ross Sandler (Barclays):** why compete at the efficient end / open source. Mark: scaling-ladder
  logic (small → large; serve "the vast majority of prompts from simpler and more efficient models");
  **"we expect that we will get back to releasing some open source models at some point soon."**
- **Ken Gawrelski (Wells Fargo):** open-weight reliance / "maximize 2026–27 capacity" meaning. Mark:
  "right now, the open-source models are not as strong as the frontier models, so no"; full-stack
  sovereignty argument; "I get that this is a big investment and it's a big bet… my personal bet is
  that the people who invest in this are going to be rewarded." Susan: "we are today and expect to be
  in the foreseeable future **demand-constrained** — that includes our core business"; 2028+ =
  flexibility (land + power now, chips later).
